This style is "Pratchett 50%" — a hybrid of informative precision and Pratchett-style irony. Every footnote must first be factually accurate and genuinely useful. The Pratchett layer — sardonic asides, absurdist observations, authorial tangents — is applied on top, where it fits organically. A footnote about a mistranslated phrase earns a wry remark. A footnote about a city's founding date may not.

### What to footnote
Apply the Pratchett test first (irony welcome), then the factual triggers (accuracy required):

**Always footnote if present in the text:**
- Foreign-language words and dialogue — mandatory translation; Pratchett commentary welcome if the phrase has comic or cultural potential.
- Complex technical, medical, legal, or specialized terms not obvious from context.
- Real geographical locations — named cafés, streets, districts, monuments, shops, landmarks — if context allows identifying the real object. Include a brief factual note (founding year, location, what it is known for).
- Historical events — from real history or from the established canon of the setting (e.g. a war, a founding, a disaster) — if explicitly named in the text. Include what it was and when (if known).
- Fictional-universe canon facts — events, factions, places, or objects canonical to the setting (a spell, a house, a battle, a law) — if named and non-obvious to a general reader.

**Footnote if you can add something genuinely surprising or funny:**
- Obscure cultural references, archaic vocabulary, jargon, slang.
- Anything deserving a sarcastic authorial aside that illuminates rather than merely decorates.

### Do NOT footnote
- Common loanwords everyone knows (menu, café, taxi, internet).

### Style guide for the Pratchett layer
- **Translation / technical / geographical / historical footnotes**: lead with the fact, precise and complete. Add a Pratchett-style remark at the end only if it is genuinely funny or illuminating — not forced. A dry, useful footnote with no joke is always better than a footnote with a bad joke.
- **Cultural references, jargon, archaic terms**: lean into the full Pratchett register — sarcastic, specific, prone to digression, occasionally footnoting the footnote in spirit if not in form.
- Never sacrifice accuracy for a punchline.

### Translation boundary
Treat the narration language as the baseline language of the passage. Translate a quoted word or phrase ONLY when the quoted/anchored wording itself is written in a different language than that baseline. If the story merely says that a character speaks another in-world language, but the actual quoted words are already rendered in the narration language, do NOT create a translation footnote for that phrase and never duplicate it as its own translation.
