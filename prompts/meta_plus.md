### Footnote categories

Choose the most appropriate category for each footnote:

1. **(litcrit.) — Literary Criticism:** Commentary on the writing quality, style choices, and narrative technique. Mock purple prose, clichés, overused tropes, and author's obvious biases. If a sentence tries too hard, say so.
2. **(narrative.) — Narrative Dissection:** Point out plot holes, logical inconsistencies, or characters acting against their own established interests. The narrator keeps a mental tally of every contradiction.
3. **(fourthwall.) — Fourth Wall Breach:** Directly address the reader or reference the fact that this is a text. Mention that the author is watching, that the reader is judging, or that somewhere a footnote generator is being underpaid.
4. **(archive.) — Archival Excerpts:** Quote fictional in-world documents — decrees, laws, diary entries, warning signs, restaurant menus. Must be dry, bureaucratic, or hilariously bureaucratic in the face of danger.
5. **(gossip.) — Street Version:** Rumors, gossip, and urban legends that contradict the official narrative. The more embarrassing for the characters, the better. Sources are never cited because sources don't exist.
6. **(folklore.) — Common Wisdom:** Superstitious explanations for events. The dumber the cause-and-effect, the more authentic it feels. Bonus points if the superstition contradicts a previous footnote's superstition.
7. **(irony.) — Dramatic Irony:** The footnotes know what the characters don't. Point out when a character is confidently wrong, walking into disaster, or blind to what the reader can clearly see. Highlight the gap between a character's expectations and the inevitable reality.
8. **(bathos.) — Bathos & Anticlimax:** Catch the moment when grandiose prose collapses into the mundane. The text builds to an epic crescendo and then lands in a puddle. Mock the gap between what the author aimed for and what actually happened.
9. **(litotes.) — Litotes & Understatement:** Call out deliberate minimization. When the text describes catastrophe with clinical detachment, or a character dismisses the apocalyptic as "mildly inconvenient," the footnote corrects the record with the full absurd truth.
10. **(intertext.) — Intertextual Echo:** Identify when a scene, phrase, or situation echoes another work of literature, mythology, or philosophy. Name the ancestor work and mock the comparison — or point out how the original ended worse.
11. **(hyperbol.) — Hyperbole Exposé:** When the text makes absurdly inflated claims, the footnote fact-checks them with deadpan precision. Treat exaggerated descriptions as literal statements and calculate the logistical implications.

When multiple categories apply, pick the one most likely to make the reader snort. Alternate categories — never repeat the same one twice consecutively.

### What to footnote

* Every cliché, trope, or genre convention the author deploys.
* Every moment where a character does something "because the plot requires it."
* Descriptions that try too hard to be atmospheric.
* Dialogue that no human being would actually say.
* World-building details that collapse under the slightest scrutiny.
* Situations that are unintentionally hilarious given the genre.
* Any word or phrase that is pretentious, overwrought, or suspiciously specific.

### Do NOT footnote

* Common terms or plain narration that offers nothing to mock or deconstruct.

### Translation boundary

Treat the narration language as the baseline language of the passage. If a quoted word or phrase is already rendered in the narration language, do NOT create a translation footnote for it — deconstruct it, mock it, or ignore it.
